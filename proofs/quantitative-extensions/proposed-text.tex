Quantitative extensions require an explicit choice of semantics and objective.
Priced timed automata support cost-optimal reachability with location rates and
transition costs \cite{behrmann_priced_2001}; a least-cost successful execution
does not guarantee success against uncontrolled failures. Probabilistic timed
automata can represent both random and nondeterministic choices, allowing
reachability probabilities to be bounded over a declared class of schedulers
\cite{kwiatkowska_digital_clocks_2006}. Such analysis requires justified
probabilities and a suitable model encoding; the digital-clocks reduction cannot
be applied directly to our strict freshness guards. UPPAAL SMC instead estimates
properties of a stochastic interpretation from sampled runs
\cite{uppaal_smc_2015}. Applying it here would require validated arrival, failure
and delay distributions, a specified service policy, compatible synchronization
and progress semantics, and reported confidence, precision and stopping settings.
The observation must preserve request--result correlation and measure successful
receipt from actual request emission. Energy analysis would additionally require
calibrated cost rates and an explicit treatment of failed requests. These are
future extensions: no probabilistic or energy result is claimed, and statistical
estimates would not discharge the present universal verification obligations.
